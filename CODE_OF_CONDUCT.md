# CyborgHeart Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We pledge to make the CyborgHeart community welcoming, safe, and equitable for everyone.

We are committed to an environment that respects the dignity, rights, and contributions of all participants, regardless of characteristics including race, ethnicity, caste, color, age, physical characteristics, neurodiversity, disability, sex or gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, language, philosophy or religion, national or social origin, socio-economic position, level of education, or other status.

The same privileges of participation are extended to everyone who participates in good faith and in accordance with this Code of Conduct.

## Encouraged behavior

We agree to act mindfully toward one another and to:

1. respect the purpose of the project and its community spaces;
2. engage kindly, honestly, and professionally;
3. respect different viewpoints and experiences;
4. take responsibility for our actions and contributions;
5. give and accept constructive feedback gracefully;
6. repair harm when it occurs;
7. credit the sources of contributed work;
8. promote the long-term well-being of the community.

## Restricted behavior

The following conduct, including threats or promotion of it, is prohibited:

1. **Harassment** — violating expressed boundaries or continuing unwanted personal attention after a clear request to stop.
2. **Character attacks** — insulting, demeaning, or pejorative comments directed at a person or group.
3. **Stereotyping or discrimination** — characterizing people on the basis of immutable identities or traits.
4. **Sexualization** — conduct that is inappropriately intimate for the project context.
5. **Violating confidentiality** — sharing or acting on private information without permission.
6. **Endangerment** — causing, encouraging, or threatening violence or other harm.
7. **Misleading identity** — impersonating another person or evading enforcement through a false identity.
8. **Failure to credit sources** — presenting another party's work as one's own.
9. **Inappropriate promotion** — commercial or promotional activity outside the norms of the project.
10. **Irresponsible communication** — presenting or linking harmful material without the care required by its context.
11. Other conduct that threatens the safety or well-being of the community.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all CyborgHeart community spaces and when a participant officially represents CyborgHeart or ProjectCyborg in public or private spaces.

Examples include repositories, issue trackers, pull requests, review discussions, chat systems, meetings, events, official accounts, and direct communication concerning project participation.

## Reporting an issue

Possible violations must be reported privately through the conduct-reporting contact published in the repository or organization metadata.

Until that private contact is published, the project is not open for unmanaged public community participation. A person who needs to report an incident may open a minimal public issue asking for private contact, but must not include incident details, identifying information, or evidence in that issue.

Before accepting public contributions, maintainers must configure and test a durable private reporting channel and identify the moderators who can access it.

Reports should include, when safe and relevant:

- a description of what occurred;
- where and when it occurred;
- links, messages, logs, or other evidence;
- people affected or able to provide context;
- any immediate safety concern;
- the reporter's preferred form of follow-up.

Moderators will treat reports seriously, investigate fairly, limit access to sensitive information, and prioritize the safety and confidentiality of affected people. Enforcement actions are normally handled privately. A public statement may be made when necessary for community safety, while minimizing disclosure of private information.

Security vulnerabilities must instead follow [`SECURITY.md`](./SECURITY.md).

## Addressing and repairing harm

When a violation is substantiated, moderators may use the following enforcement ladder. They may skip steps when the severity or safety impact requires it.

### 1. Warning

**Event:** A single incident or a limited series of incidents.

**Consequence:** A private written warning explaining the behavior and the expected correction.

**Possible repair:** Acknowledgement of responsibility, clarification of expectations, and a private or mutually agreed apology.

### 2. Temporarily limited activities

**Event:** Repeated conduct after a warning, or a first incident with greater impact.

**Consequence:** A time-limited restriction on specified project spaces, activities, or interactions.

**Possible repair:** Respecting the restriction, reflecting on impact, and following stated conditions for return.

### 3. Temporary suspension

**Event:** A serious violation or a pattern that earlier measures did not resolve.

**Consequence:** Temporary removal from project participation, with written conditions for return where return is considered safe.

**Possible repair:** Meeting the stated conditions and re-entering the community thoughtfully after the suspension.

### 4. Permanent ban

**Event:** A severe violation or a pattern for which lesser measures cannot keep the community safe.

**Consequence:** Permanent removal from CyborgHeart community spaces, tools, and communication channels under project control.

Permanent bans should be rare, documented privately, and based on clear safety reasoning.

This ladder guides rather than limits moderator judgment. Decisions should be proportionate, consistent, and focused on community safety and repair.

## Moderator responsibilities

Project moderators are responsible for:

- receiving and safeguarding reports;
- avoiding conflicts of interest;
- investigating relevant evidence;
- documenting decisions privately;
- applying proportionate action consistently;
- communicating outcomes to involved parties when safe;
- recusing themselves when impartiality is reasonably in doubt.

Maintainers may remove, edit, or reject contributions and may restrict access when required to enforce this Code of Conduct.
